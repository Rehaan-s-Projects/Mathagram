📊 Read: A Sine Wave

For \(y = A\sin(Bx + C) + D\): \(A\) is the amplitude (height from midline), \(\frac{2\pi}{|B|}\) is the period, \(-C/B\) is the phase shift, and \(D\) is the vertical shift.

📊 Read: Cosine vs Sine

Cosine is sine shifted left by \(\pi/2\): \(\cos x = \sin\bigl(x + \tfrac{\pi}{2}\bigr)\). Both have amplitude 1 and period \(2\pi\) — they just start at different heights.

📊 Read: Stretching & Squeezing

For \(y = 3\sin(2x)\): the coefficient \(3\) triples the amplitude, and the coefficient \(2\) halves the period to \(\pi\). The graph oscillates between \(-3\) and \(+3\), completing a full cycle in \(\pi\) units.
