What Is a Talker Machine?

Augmentative & Alternative Communication (AAC)

Rita Hi! I'm Rita. Some people can't use their voice to speak — so they use a talker machine. It's a device that turns button presses, eye gaze, or typing into spoken words. Let's learn how one works!
Step 1 of 4

What is a Talker Machine?

A talker machine — formally called an AAC device (Augmentative and Alternative Communication) — is any tool that helps a person who has trouble speaking. They can be:

• Low-tech (paper letter boards, picture binders)
• Mid-tech (single-button voice recorders)
• High-tech (tablets running Proloquo2Go, TouchChat, LAMP, or full speech-generating devices with eye-tracking)

Step 2 of 4

Who uses a talker machine?

People of all ages — anyone whose voice doesn't work the way they need it to.

Stephen HawkingFamous physicist; ALS left him unable to speak. Used a single cheek-switch + DECtalk synthesizer for 30+ years.
Children with autism~30% are minimally verbal. Apps like Proloquo2Go give them a voice.
People with ALSLose speech as the disease progresses. Eye-tracking AAC keeps them communicating.
Stroke survivorsAphasia can take away words. Symbol boards rebuild bridges.
Cerebral palsySpeech can be hard to produce; switches & symbol grids work around it.
Many moreDown syndrome, brain injury, Rett syndrome — AAC is for anyone who needs another way to talk.
