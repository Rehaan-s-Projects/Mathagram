Unit 1 — Electricity Basics

Electric Charge

Electric charge is the fundamental property of matter that creates electrical forces. Every proton carries a positive charge, every electron carries a negative charge, and neutrons carry no charge at all.

Two rules that govern every electrical interaction in the universe:

  • Like charges repel. Two positives push each other apart; two negatives push each other apart.
  • Opposite charges attract. A positive and a negative pull toward each other.

The strength of that force was measured by Charles-Augustin de Coulomb in 1785. Coulomb's Law says the force between two point charges \(q_1\) and \(q_2\) separated by distance \(r\) is:

$$F = k\,\frac{q_1\,q_2}{r^2}$$

where \(k \approx 8.99 \times 10^{9}\ \text{N·m}^2/\text{C}^2\) is Coulomb's constant. Doubling the distance cuts the force to a quarter — it's an inverse-square law, just like gravity.

The smallest unit — the elementary charge. Every electron carries a charge of exactly

$$e \approx 1.602 \times 10^{-19}\ \text{C}$$

and every proton carries \(+e\). Charge is quantized — it only ever comes in whole-number multiples of \(e\). You can have one electron's worth, two electrons' worth, a billion electrons' worth — but never half an electron's worth. One coulomb is roughly 6.24 × 10¹⁸ electrons — that's a lot.

Charge is conserved. You cannot create or destroy net charge — only move it around. Rub a balloon on your hair and the balloon steals electrons from the hair. The balloon becomes negative, the hair becomes positive, but the total charge of (balloon + hair) is exactly what it was before.

Three ways to charge an object:

  • Friction — rubbing two materials together transfers electrons. The triboelectric series ranks materials by how strongly they grab electrons (human hair gives them up easily; rubber and PVC grab them eagerly).
  • Conduction — touch a charged object to a neutral one. Charge spreads until both share it.
  • Induction — bring a charged object near a neutral one without touching. The neutral object's own charges rearrange — opposites pulled close, likes pushed away. Ground the far side and you can leave it permanently charged of the opposite sign.

Why this matters for circuits. Every voltage, every current, every spark in every device on Earth is just this happening at scale — charge being separated by a power source, then driven through a wire to recombine. Electric charge is the currency electricity spends.
