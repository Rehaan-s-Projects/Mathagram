Unit 1 — Story Mode

🍕 Diego's Pizza Optimization

Diego owns a pizza shop and has a pricing problem. Read along with him and Rita as they use calculus to find the perfect price. Answer the questions in each scene to advance the story.

📖 Scene 1 · The Problem

It's a slow Tuesday at Slice Lighthouse Pizza. Diego stares at his sales spreadsheet and sighs. His friend Rita walks in.

D
Diego
Rita, help! I can't figure out what to charge per pizza. If the price is too low, I lose money on each pie. If it's too high, customers go elsewhere.
R
Rita
Classic optimization problem. Tell me your numbers — how does the number of pizzas you sell depend on the price?
D
Diego
At price p dollars per pizza, I sell about \((50 - 2p)\) pizzas. So my revenue is the price times the number sold: \(R(p) = (50 - 2p) \cdot p\).
Revenue function: \(R(p) = (50 - 2p)\,p = 50p - 2p^2\)
📖 Scene 2 · The Setup
R
Rita
Perfect. Now to find the maximum revenue, take the derivative and set it equal to zero. That gives you the critical points — the candidates for the peak.
D
Diego
Okay. \(R(p) = 50p - 2p^2\). The derivative of \(50p\) is 50, and the derivative of \(-2p^2\) is \(-4p\). So \(R'(p) = 50 - 4p\).
Derivative: \(R'(p) = 50 - 4p\)
R
Rita
Great. Now set \(R'(p) = 0\) and solve for p. That's where the slope of the revenue curve is flat — the top of the hill.
📖 Scene 3 · The Solution
D
Diego
\(50 - 4p = 0\) → \(4p = 50\) → \(p = 12.5\). So… the magic price is $12.50?
R
Rita
Almost! We have a critical point, but we need to check if it's actually a maximum, not a minimum. Compute the second derivative and look at its sign.
D
Diego
\(R'(p) = 50 - 4p\), so \(R''(p) = -4\). That's negative everywhere — the curve is concave down.
Second derivative test: \(R''(p) = -4 < 0\) → the critical point \(p = 12.5\) is a local maximum. ✅
R
Rita
Confirmed! \(p = 12.50\) maximizes your revenue. Now plug it back in to see how much money you actually make.
📖 Scene 4 · The Payoff
D
Diego
\(R(12.5) = 50(12.5) - 2(12.5)^2 = 625 - 312.5 = \$312.50\). I'll make $312.50 every day at the optimal price!
Maximum daily revenue: \(R(12.5) = \$312.50\)

Sam, a regular customer, walks in, sniffs the air, and grins.

S
Sam
Wait — you raised the price to $12.50 and your total revenue went up? That's wild.
R
Rita
That's the whole point of calculus optimization, Sam. Sometimes you sell fewer units but make more total revenue. The derivative finds the sweet spot.
D
Diego
Math just paid for my new pizza oven. 🍕📈
