What Is the IPA?

The International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) is a single, universal system for writing down the sounds of any spoken language. Created by the International Phonetic Association in 1888, it gives every distinct sound its own symbol — no matter what alphabet the language uses.

English spelling alone is famously unreliable. The letter "a" sounds different in cat, father, gate, sofa. The IPA fixes that: those four sounds get four different symbols — /æ/, /ɑ/, /eɪ/, /ə/ — and they mean exactly the same thing whether you're transcribing English, Mandarin, Swahili, or Quechua.
